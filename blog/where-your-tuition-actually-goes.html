Where Your Tuition Actually Goes

October 29, 2026

Parents never ask me this directly, but I know plenty wonder, so let me just answer it. Our average tuition is about 1,550 dollars a month. Here is where it actually goes.

Start with the two biggest pieces. Every classroom in our building costs about 11,000 dollars a month in rent and salaries alone, before a single meal is served or a single material is placed on a shelf. An infant room holds 10 children, a toddler room 16, and primary rooms in the low twenties, so depending on the room, those two line items alone consume most of the tuition a classroom brings in.

Then come the pieces nobody sees. Our property tax bill is about 45,000 dollars a year, and most of it goes to the public school district, which now runs its own pre-K programs competing for the very children we serve. Liability insurance is its own story. We found exactly two companies in the state of Texas willing to insure us, and we only qualify with ours because our elementary program lets them classify us as a school. The other option costs about four times more, and our premium has climbed roughly 30 percent year over year, because insurers started pulling out of Texas childcare around 2021. My contractor even lost his own insurance after disclosing that he sometimes performs work for a school.

Then the small stuff that is never small. Toilet paper is 12 dollars a roll for the commercial kind, and a box of trifold paper towels jumped from 21 dollars to 29 in a year. The plumber comes almost monthly at 400 dollars because toddlers and toilets are natural enemies. An AC service pops up at 1,500. The fire alarm needed a 2,000 dollar fix because only one company in the area can service the system. A child once threw a rock at a classroom window, and that was 2,250 dollars. This is a new building. Every month I plan, prep, and review the numbers, and every month something new arrives that costs hundreds or thousands and cannot wait until enrollment catches up to cover it.

Salaries deserve their own honest paragraph. Two years ago, lead teachers in this area were asking for 45,000 to 55,000 dollars. Today 55,000 is often the floor, even without a credential, and credentialing a teacher ourselves runs another 15,000 to 19,000 on top. That is not elementary pay. That is infant and preschool.

And when there is anything left over, it goes back into the building. Our turf playground was 100,000 dollars, installed in 2023 with the help of a grant. Our Montessori materials are all being replaced over the next two years. We have always reinvested, because the leftover is not profit sitting somewhere, it is the next improvement waiting its turn.

Through all of it, we have chosen to keep tuition increases as low as we possibly can, because we want this school to stay within reach of real families. So if you have ever wondered why childcare owners do this work, I can promise you it is not for the money, and if you are ever curious how I personally pay my bills, you should probably ask my husband, because it is certainly not my school. What 1,550 dollars actually buys is every day with your child in the hands of people who genuinely try their best to love them, care for them, and teach them, and honestly, knowing everything it takes to keep these doors open, I think that might be the best deal in Texas.
